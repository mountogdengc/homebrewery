<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody><CharStyle:Italic>The Vault Below Thornhall<CharStyle:> is a four-part solo adventure for the Cascade roleplaying game. You don't need a Storyguide. <ParaStyle:Headings:Heading3>Understanding the Tiers
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody>You just learned how Cascade rolls work. Here's the pattern you'll use every time:
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody>The TN is the threshold for <CharStyle:Bold>Clean Success<CharStyle:>: you got exactly what you set out to do.
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody><CharStyle:Bold>One tier above<CharStyle:> (TN + 2) is <CharStyle:Bold>Success Plus<CharStyle:>, meaning you got more than expected.
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody><CharStyle:Bold>One tier below<CharStyle:> (TN <0x2013> 2) is <CharStyle:Bold>Success at Cost<CharStyle:>. You got what you wanted, but something else went wrong. The SG introduces a complication.
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody><CharStyle:Bold>More than one tier below<CharStyle:> is <CharStyle:Bold>Failure<CharStyle:>: you didn't get it, and consequences follow.
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody>The tiers are always spaced two apart. A TN 12 task succeeds on 12, has a cost on 10<0x2013>11, succeeds plus on 14+, and fails below 10.

<ParaStyle:Headings:Heading3>A Second Roll
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody>Here you face a choice not just of action but of <CharStyle:Bold>approach<CharStyle:>. The same goal, getting past this guard, can be attempted different ways. The attribute you use shapes both the roll and the story.
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody><CharStyle:Bold>Talking your way out<CharStyle:> uses <CharStyle:Bold>Influence<CharStyle:> or <CharStyle:Bold>Poise<CharStyle:>. You're trying to convince him this isn't worth his trouble, or that you have a good reason to be here.
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody><CharStyle:Bold>Getting past him physically<CharStyle:> uses <CharStyle:Bold>Reflex<CharStyle:>. You're looking for the moment to slip past before he can react.

<ParaStyle:Headings:Heading3>The Luck Die
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody>Every Cascade roll uses three dice. Two are your <CharStyle:Bold>Result Dice<CharStyle:>, and their total, plus your attribute, determines your outcome. The third die is different.
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody>Before every roll, set one die apart. Make it distinct: different color, different position, turned on its side. This is the <CharStyle:Bold>Luck Die.<CharStyle:>
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody>After the dice land, the Luck Die shows a number. You can see it. It might be high; it might be low. You have a choice about what to do with it.
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody><CharStyle:Bold>Luck Tokens<CharStyle:> are a resource. Write <CharStyle:Bold>"Luck: 1"<CharStyle:> at the top of your character sheet. Most starting characters have 1 Luck token at the beginning of each session.
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody><CharStyle:Bold>Spending a Luck Token:<CharStyle:> After rolling, you may spend 1 token to swap one of your Result Dice with the Luck Die. You choose which Result Die to replace. The Luck Die's number takes its place, and the old die's number is gone.
<ParaStyle:Body Text:CoreBody>This is always an informed decision. You see all three numbers before you choose. If the Luck Die shows a 5 and one of your Result Dice shows a 1, the math speaks for itself. If the Luck Die shows a 2 and both Result Dice are decent, save your token.
