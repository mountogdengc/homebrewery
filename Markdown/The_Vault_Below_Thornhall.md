# THE VAULT BELOW THORNHALL
## A Cascade Solo Adventure Arc

*Mount Ogden Gaming Company*

---

## About This Adventure

*The Vault Below Thornhall* is a four-part solo adventure for the Cascade roleplaying game. You don't need a Storyguide. You don't need other players. You need a pencil, three six-sided dice (ideally one that's a different color from the other two) and a few pieces of paper.

This adventure teaches you how Cascade works by putting you in the middle of a story and asking you to act. Every part introduces one new piece of the game, and by the end, you'll know the core of how Cascade plays.

Start with Part One. You won't need any dice yet.

# PART ONE: A SIMPLE JOB
*In which you learn what kind of world this is.*

---

Merchant Vesna Tholl is not a patient woman. She found you at the Cracked Antler, the only tavern in Thornhall still warm this late in the season, and made her offer in short, no-nonsense sentences.

Someone stole her ledger. Not coins. Not jewelry. A ledger: the kind with names in it that her business partners would prefer stay hidden.

The thief, a fence named Corvin Ash, has taken it into the vault beneath the old border wall. The one locals avoid talking about.

She'll pay well if you bring it back. She'll pay nothing if you don't.

That's how you end up in front of a rusted iron door set into the base of Thornhall's oldest wall, lantern in hand, cold autumn rain on your back.

# PART TWO: A NAME AND A NUMBER
*In which you discover who you are, and what you're capable of.*

---

### Who Are You?

You've decided to go back. Before you do, let's figure out who exactly is walking into that vault.

Take a piece of paper. At the top, write a name. It can be your own, a name from a story you like, or something you invent right now. Any name works.

Below the name, write one sentence about their background: what they did before they ended up in Thornhall. A soldier between wars. A ship's surgeon far from any coast. A courier who noticed too many messages going missing. A hired blade looking for cleaner work. Write something that feels right.

Now let's find out how capable they are.

---

### The Nine Attributes

Your character has nine attributes organized across three domains. Each attribute is a number from 1 to 5 that describes how good they are in that area. Most starting characters sit between 1 and 3.

Write these on your character sheet:

**Physical Domain**

| Attribute | What it governs |
|---|---|
| Might | Force, raw power, physical imposition |
| Reflex | Speed, precision, agility |
| Endurance | Resilience; also determines how much damage you can take |

**Mental Domain**

| Attribute | What it governs |
|---|---|
| Reason | Analysis, knowledge, intellectual force |
| Guile | Cunning, deception, misdirection |
| Resolve | Mental fortitude; how much psychological pressure you can endure |

**Social Domain**

| Attribute | What it governs |
|---|---|
| Influence | Presence, persuasion |
| Poise | Grace, adaptability |
| Command | Authority; also governs social staying power |

### Assigning Your Attributes

You have these nine scores to distribute: **3, 3, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1**

Assign one number to each attribute. Think about the character you described. A soldier will want high Might and Endurance. A former physician might want high Reason and Guile. A courier might want balanced Reflex and Reason. There's no wrong answer; match the numbers to the person.

Once you've assigned all nine scores, continue with **section 1**.

And the numbers aren't transactions. They're payments. Vesna Tholl built a network, and that ledger is the record of what she paid to protect it.*

*The vault is darker than before. Your lantern throws a small circle of yellow light. The storeroom where you found Corvin is empty, the table overturned, the lantern gone. Someone else has been through here.*

- If you search the room carefully before moving on, continue with **section 2**.
- If you move quickly through to the corridor beyond, continue with **section 3**.

## 2.

---

### How to Make a Roll

Pick up all three dice. Before you roll, set one aside and make it visually distinct: turn it sideways, or use a different color if you have one. This is your **Luck Die**. The other two are your **Result Dice**.

Roll all three. Then:

1. Add your two **Result Dice** together.
2. Add the relevant **attribute** for what you're doing.
3. Compare the total to a **Target Number (TN)** set before the roll.

For this search, you're looking for patterns and things out of place; that's your **Reason** attribute at work. The Target Number is **TN 10**. Searching a dark, disturbed room takes real attention, making this a moderate challenge.

**After you roll**, look at all three dice. Keep your Luck Die result in mind; you'll learn what it does in Part Three.

**Compare your total (Result Dice + Reason) to TN 10:**

| Total | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 12 or higher | **Success Plus** → Continue with **section 4** |
| 10 or 11 | **Clean Success** → Continue with **section 5** |
| 8 or 9 | **Success at Cost** → Continue with **section 6** |
| 7 or lower | **Failure** → Continue with **section 7** |

*The room gives up nothing useful. You've spent time you didn't have, and when you finally move on, you're doing so with less information and a nagging feeling you missed something.*

### Understanding the Tiers

You just learned how Cascade rolls work. Here's the pattern you'll use every time:

The TN is the threshold for **Clean Success**: you got exactly what you set out to do.

**One tier above** (TN + 2) is **Success Plus**, meaning you got more than expected.

**One tier below** (TN – 2) is **Success at Cost**. You got what you wanted, but something else went wrong. The SG introduces a complication.

**More than one tier below** is **Failure**: you didn't get it, and consequences follow.

The tiers are always spaced two apart. A TN 12 task succeeds on 12, has a cost on 10–11, succeeds plus on 14+, and fails below 10.

### A Second Roll

Here you face a choice not just of action but of **approach**. The same goal, getting past this guard, can be attempted different ways. The attribute you use shapes both the roll and the story.

**Talking your way out** uses **Influence** or **Poise**. You're trying to convince him this isn't worth his trouble, or that you have a good reason to be here.

**Getting past him physically** uses **Reflex**. You're looking for the moment to slip past before he can react.

Choose your approach. The **Target Number is TN 12**, a genuine challenge either way.

Roll 3d6. Add your Result Dice and your chosen attribute.

| Total | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 14 or higher | **Success Plus** → Continue with **section 13** |
| 12 or 13 | **Clean Success** → Continue with **section 14** |
| 10 or 11 | **Success at Cost** → Continue with **section 15** |
| 9 or lower | **Failure** → Continue with **section 16** |

# PART THREE: THE THIRD DIE
*In which you learn what fortune costs, and what it's worth.*

---

### The Luck Die

Every Cascade roll uses three dice. Two are your **Result Dice**, and their total, plus your attribute, determines your outcome. The third die is different.

Before every roll, set one die apart. Make it distinct: different color, different position, turned on its side. This is the **Luck Die.**

After the dice land, the Luck Die shows a number. You can see it. It might be high; it might be low. You have a choice about what to do with it.

**Luck Tokens** are a resource. Write **"Luck: 1"** at the top of your character sheet. Most starting characters have 1 Luck token at the beginning of each session.

**Spending a Luck Token:** After rolling, you may spend 1 token to swap one of your Result Dice with the Luck Die. You choose which Result Die to replace. The Luck Die's number takes its place, and the old die's number is gone.

This is always an informed decision. You see all three numbers before you choose. If the Luck Die shows a 5 and one of your Result Dice shows a 1, the math speaks for itself. If the Luck Die shows a 2 and both Result Dice are decent, save your token.

**The other side of it:** If you choose not to spend, or you've already spent your token, the Storyguide can spend from their own resource pool (called **Taint**) to force a swap instead. The SG takes the die that makes your result *worse*. In a normal game this is the adversarial edge of the mechanic. In this solo adventure, we'll call it out directly when it would happen.

Write down your Luck token. You'll use it before this part is over.

## 10.

---

### Using the Luck Die

This is your first chance to use the Luck Die deliberately. Here's how this roll works:

Pick up all three dice. Set your Luck Die aside visibly. Roll all three.

You can try to **pick the lock** (using **Reflex**, TN 12) or **find another way through** (using **Reason**, TN 10).

Decide first, then roll. Look at all three dice.

**Before you compare your total:** Look at the Luck Die. This is the moment. You have 1 Luck token.

- If the Luck Die is **higher** than one of your Result Dice, you might want to swap. Spend your token, replace the lower Result Die with the Luck Die, and your total improves.
- If the Luck Die is **lower** than both Result Dice, don't swap. Your token is still yours.
- If you're unsure, think about how close you are to the TN. Even 1 point can change your tier.

**Now finalize your total** (with or without the swap) and compare:

*Picking the lock (Reflex, TN 12):*

| Total | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 14+ | **Success Plus** → **Section 13** |
| 12–13 | **Clean Success** → **Section 14** |
| 10–11 | **Success at Cost** → **Section 15** |
| 9 or lower | **Failure** → **Section 16** |

*Finding another way (Reason, TN 10):*

| Total | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 12+ | **Success Plus** → **Section 17** |
| 10–11 | **Clean Success** → **Section 14** |
| 8–9 | **Success at Cost** → **Section 15** |
| 7 or lower | **Failure** → **Section 16** |

## 15.

# PART FOUR: BLOOD ON THE STONE
*In which you learn what it costs to fight, and what it wins.*

---

### Your Vitality Track

In Cascade, physical harm is tracked on your **Vitality track**. Calculate yours now:

**Vitality = 3 + Endurance**

If your Endurance is 2, your Vitality track has 5 boxes. Draw them in a row on your character sheet.

As you take damage, mark boxes from left to right. Three tiers:

| When | Condition | Penalty to all rolls |
|---|---|---|
| Half your boxes empty | Injured | –1 |
| Only 1–2 boxes left | Wounded | –2 |
| Zero boxes | Felled | –3, and only 1 action per round |

Being Felled is not death. It's a threshold, a significant story moment. The adventure continues.

Also note your **Physical Defense TN: 8 + Endurance.** Enemies attack against this number. When they act, you'll roll to see if their attack succeeds against your Defense TN. (The adventure will explain each time.)

---

### Conflict: The Basics

When a situation escalates into structured opposition, whether a fight or a standoff that turns physical, you enter **conflict**. Conflict runs in **rounds**. Each round, you act once. On your turn, you have **3 actions**.

**Common actions:**

- **Attack** — Roll Might or Reflex + skill vs. the enemy's Physical Defense TN. On success, deal weapon damage. Unarmed attacks deal 1 damage.
- **Defend** — Raise your Physical Defense TN by 2 until your next turn.
- **Move** — Change your position. Usually free if uncontested.
- **Assess** — Ask one honest question about the scene: "Who's the biggest threat?" "Is there a way out?" The answer is always true.
- **Recover** — Clear one Tier 1 condition (Injured) if you have a genuine moment of rest.

**The Repeat Penalty:** Using the same action type twice in a round costs –2 on the second roll. A third use costs –4.

**NPCs Never Roll.** When an enemy attacks you, *you* roll to defend. When you attack an enemy, *you* roll against their Defense TN. The dice are always in your hands.

**Mooks** are minor opponents: common guards, hired muscle. A mook group has:

- **Threshold** — the damage needed to take one down
- **Size** — how many are in the group

Each time you deal damage equal to or exceeding the Threshold in a single hit, reduce Size by 1. When Size reaches 0, they're done.

When a mook group gets to act (if you haven't Defended), each remaining mook deals its listed damage to your Vitality.

**(Your Turn First)**

**Option: Attack, Defend, Assess**

## What You've Learned

Across this arc, you played through the core of how Cascade works:

**Part One** showed you that the game lives in decision-making. The world responds to what you do, and no dice are required to tell a good story.

**Part Two** introduced the Roll: three dice, two for your result, one kept apart. Attributes define your approach, Target Numbers set the difficulty, and tiers (success plus, clean success, cost, failure) shape how outcomes land.

**Part Three** gave you the Luck Die as a real resource. Informed decisions, real tradeoffs. The Luck Die is always visible, always in play, always potentially yours or the SG's.

**Part Four** showed you conflict: structured rounds, 3 actions, the repeat penalty, NPCs that never roll, Vitality as a track rather than a number, and mooks that go down in groups.

You're ready for a full Cascade campaign.

If you want to continue this story with a Storyguide and other players, everything you built here (your character, your attributes, your Vitality track, the contacts you've made and the enemies you've earned) carries forward.
